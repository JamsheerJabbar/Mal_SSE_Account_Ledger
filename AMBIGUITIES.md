If closing balance for a day was negative and created an overdraft fee, then later on a transaction was reversed and added interest, then both are existing for a same day which is ambigous
If day2 transaction is reverted or updated on day 8, we will have to recalculate the interest and revert the old total interest. This can also increase cycles on day 2 while calculating for day8.

Rounding the daily interest accrual - if we round the day's accrual down we undercharge by a fraction, if we round half-up we can end up crediting interest that was never actually earned (465 x 0.0004 = 0.186, which is 0.18 rounded down but 0.19 rounded half-up, and Notes.md's own worked total needs the 0.18 answer). Picked round-down for this reason.

When the daily accruals are rounded before being added to the tally, the sum of those rounded dailies does not have to equal what you'd get by recomputing the total fresh from the raw unrounded daily figures and rounding once at the end - one gives 0.76, the other gives 0.77 on the same example. Picked summing the already-rounded dailies, so the capitalized credit always reconciles back to the stored accrual history instead of being some other number nobody can trace.

Whether the capitalization day's own accrual should be included in the total it credits is circular - the balance that day's accrual is based on already includes the credit being computed from it. Excluded the capitalization day's own accrual from the total; Notes.md's own figures for both accounts only add up if that day is left out.

Notes.md is not consistent with itself on whether a day with no transactions of its own should still get assessed for overdraft: the prose says the fee "carries on to day4 and day 5", but the worked closing balance for day 5 (-205) and the day-6 reversal count (exactly two, for day2 and day4) only add up if day 5 is NOT separately charged. Went with what the numbers say over what the prose says, since the numbers are checkable and the prose isn't.

Whether a day is judged on its balance before or after its own overdraft fee used to change whether the fee could trap itself - if judged after its own fee, a day sitting at +10 that gets a -25 fee reads as -15 and can never be seen as solvent again even once it should be, so the fee would never reverse. This stopped being a live ambiguity once overdraft fees were made permanent (never reversed at all, under any reading - see REJECTED.md R2): now neither reading can ever un-charge a fee, so the choice only changes what a day's assessmentBalance displays once it already carries one, not what happens.

Rounding each installment slice up (or half-up) can push the total paid out above what was actually credited - splitting 1.000 BHD three ways at half-up would post amounts that sum to more than 1.000. Round each slice down instead and post whatever is left over as its own separate entry, so a split can never pay out more than the total.

Whether a hold should only count from its own value date (and a settled hold keep counting until its settlement date) or should count purely on being approved, with no date involved at all. Went with pure approval status, no date constraint - but because the daily projection is fully rebuilt from the week start on every reconciliation, this means a day's displayed holds now reflect whichever holds are *currently* approved, not what was actually true on that day historically: a hold can show up on an earlier day's row before it was even raised, and disappear from that same row again once it later settles.
